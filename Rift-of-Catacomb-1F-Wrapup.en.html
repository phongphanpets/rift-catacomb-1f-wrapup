Lore / prologue

Rift of Catacomb — A prologue to Rift of Death

Narrative Draft — expanded narrative: Cosmic Horror / Lovecraftian tone. Added details, dialogue and incidents are review proposals, not locked canon or new implementation requirements.

Rift of Catacomb is a sub-location of Rift of Death, serving as an introduction to it. On entering this area, players do not yet know what Rift of Death is or what happened there.

The introduction deliberately retains the question: What is Rift of Death, and what happened there? The story below proposes unease through the catacomb, names and memory, rather than supplying the answer.

◇The Sepulchre That Remembers

The chamber of those not yet forgotten

In Master Vesper's mission chamber there is always the sound of turning pages, even when his hands lie motionless on the desk and no one can see the paper moving.

The apprentice reapers once attributed it to a draught from the ritual gate, until someone noticed that the candle flames never leaned toward the sound.

Vesper examines the names of the dead line by line. Patiently, he separates accurate records from those requiring another inspection. Certain pages, however, he folds face down as soon as he encounters them. There is no rejection stamp, no explanation, and no permission to make a copy.

Assignments from the Catacomb tend to arrive on pages of this latter kind.

Asked where the place lies, Vesper says only that it belongs to Rift of Death. The name leaves the questioner little wiser. A map can locate the catacomb; the ritual gate can deliver a traveller there. What the Rift itself might be is not explained in the papers provided.

Before sending an apprentice away, he offers a warning shorter than the assignment itself.

“If you find a name you cannot remember, bring it back. If you find one you remember, first be certain where you remember it from.”

Names upon stone

From its entrance, the Catacomb resembles an abandoned burial ground: damp walls, broken pillars, shrines long without a keeper. It is not their age that arrests the visitor, but evidence of repair. Fresh bricks patch sections above stone almost crumbled into earth, as though someone still tends a place from which nobody claims to have returned.

Nameplates lie scattered along the paths. Some remain legible; others have been scored deep into the stone. Yet the scratches do not erase every part of the lettering. They stop at the final syllable too often to seem accidental damage.

An explorer copied a name from a plate into his notebook. Looking back to check it, he discovered a space in the middle of what he had written. He remembered how certain he had felt while writing. The sound of the missing syllable would no longer come to him.

That evening he added a note blaming exhaustion. By morning, he could not remember why he had used someone else's handwriting for the note.

Not every account describes such incidents. Some visitors return with all their plates and ordinary explanations. Others insist the stones were blank from the beginning. These incompatible reports are filed together; no examiner has crossed out one to certify another.

What the light reveals

Lighting a shrine should be simple work. Light reveals the path, the names upon the stones, and the faces of those standing together.

Yet one returning visitor reported more shadows on the wall than people beside the flame. He raised a hand to discover which shadow belonged to him. Most followed the movement. One had already raised its hand.

In retelling it, he became uncertain whether he had seen that shadow move first or merely mistaken a companion's shadow for his own. The account ended in laughter. Nobody wished to be the first to ask that the flame be extinguished so they could count again.

The spirits lingering in the catacomb give no clearer answers than the shadows. Some stretches of their lament sound like attempts to pronounce the same name repeatedly. Farther within, skeletal figures still lift their weapons as though obeying an order whose giver is no longer present.

What they guard, whom they defend, or whether they merely repeat the last thing they remember: no explorer has sufficient evidence to say.

A record no author will attest

One copy of an old burial register preserves neat columns of names, dates and those receiving the bodies, until a page where every line of ink fades at once. Water cannot account for it: the ruled table and fingerprints along the edge remain clear.

Beneath the table, a sentence has been forced into the margin with enough pressure nearly to tear the paper.

“We can still count how many have died. We no longer know what to call them.”

The next page contains no ritual instructions, map or explanation of events. Only names, copied person by person. The handwriting changes several times, but each hand struggles to imitate the first line, as if restoring a name's exact appearance might suffice to restore everything else.

The register does not say whether the attempt succeeded. Its final page was torn out before delivery, and nobody knows whether sender or recipient removed it.

Vesper does not endorse the theory that forgotten names keep spirits trapped. He examines the handwriting and seals, then files the copy in a category never returned to apprentices for reading. Asked about that category, he first turns to inspect the stability of the gate.

Beyond the name of a place

Deeper within, the traveller's work still has comprehensible limits: recover what is missing, light the shrines, open a route and confront the Gravewarden. These tasks can be accomplished. Names can be returned. Those who come back are entitled to say their assignment is complete.

But no document promises that defeating the Gravewarden will answer the question of Rift of Death. No evidence establishes it as this place's creator, or proves that the entrance it guards exists to keep outsiders from coming in.

Some returning travellers no longer ask about that. They only request that Vesper read the assignee's name aloud, then check whether the name they hear still matches the one they remember.

And in the mission chamber, the sound of turning pages continues.

Although this time every book upon the desk is closed.

Beat Entrance — understandable work in an unfamiliar world

The party enters safety and talks to the NPC. Their initial goal is actionable work, not solving Rift of Death. If quest presentation is clear, they may accept the whole set; if they notice only a single quest, accepting it alone is allowed, but they will eventually need the others.

Specified response: accept separately or together; counting starts after acceptance; all turn-ins unlock the next set. Failure case: the actual quest screen is unavailable, so noticing missing quests and understanding the set gate cannot be established. See Quest Line.

Beat Blue-left — choosing overlapping work

The team map shows a left shrine, nameplates and ghosts; it is not a confirmed player-facing minimap. If quest information is sufficient, the party may visit the shrine first as recommended, or hunt ghosts while exploring. Passive ghosts should not initiate combat merely because someone passes.

Choosing to attack can advance kill and drop objectives together, but quest drops require manual pickup. Members need the relevant quest and credit eligibility. Specified response: everyone collects distinct nameplates and interacts with the shrine personally, then returns to the entrance NPC. A friend's progress does not replace their own. Possible experience: efficient overlap, or repetitive collection if personal progress is unclear. Failure case: sufficient blue-left nameplate anchors remain unverified. See Quest rules.

Beat Checkpoint route — learning to read enemies

After turning in the first set, the party takes the next set and detours right because the broken floor is impassable, visiting the shrines and fighting Skeletons along the way. Moving sideways answers the sword/shield frontal as specified. Cover or Interrupt can answer the Mage when class tools / geometry support them; not every party is assumed to have Interrupt.

If objectives are complete, members activate Checkpoint personally and talk to the NPC for turn-in; passing through is insufficient. Failure case: someone who forgets activation or turns in only some quests has different progress from friends. Actual status feedback needs inspection. Echo is not assumed to compensate for taking every attack. See Combat / Echo.

Beat Red-left — pursuit is not the only answer

The party starts inner exploration and encounters Tarantula. At 60% HP or below, it retreats to B once while remaining vulnerable. Without advance knowledge of B, players may pursue because party HP is healthy, or pause to inspect the route rather than carry combat into another pack.

If B is occupied, Mage + Melee + Archer start grouped as drafted. Prioritizing Elite / cleaving adds is a tactic to explore, not a confirmed class capability. If B was cleared, an empty destination is an accepted advantage; no replacement monsters are spawned to negate it. Failure case: retreat and Pool readability amid several enemies, and Aggro Link, have not passed a Demo. See Skull Tarantula.

Beat Side paths — fight again or uncover forgotten records

If players find Lost Chamber in blue and understand its clue, they may inspect records without another fight. An already-open door may give discovery without personally solving the puzzle. Optional Rite in red allows preparation and calling help before activation, but actual placement and discovery timing are unknown.

Draft response: both can be skipped without blocking Main Quest. Rite requires all event monsters defeated and seals closed; it does not require sealing during combat. Failure case: killing everything first may always be easier, and a single most-attractive event site may concentrate rather than distribute players. See Optional Activities.

Beat Return to Checkpoint — personal boss access

Each member must turn in the exploration set before accepting Fall of the Gravewarden, receiving a seal and using it personally at the gate. A leader opening access should not carry members past their own quest conditions.

Specified response: personal access persists through death; accepting the quest or using the seal does not spawn a new boss. Failure case: the boss may already be engaged or absent. Actual waiting depends on undefined Spawn / Respawn rules. Not every party is assumed to find a ready encounter, and Large Skeleton Elite's skills do not substitute for an unresolved Boss Spec. See pending decisions.

Beat End of prologue — clear progress, incomplete answers

If the party participates within credit conditions and defeats the boss, members return to Checkpoint to finish the Quest Line. Someone dead in the area may remain eligible; respawning outside credit range does not qualify. A party victory does not guarantee identical personal progress.

Narratively, the intended ending completes actionable work while leaving Rift of Death uncertain. Names and records may reinforce that theme once delivery is chosen. Not everyone is assumed to read Lore or feel the same dread.

◇Read this first

Rift of Catacomb is a Ragnarok-inspired Shared Dungeon, not an Instance Dungeon. Floor 1 targets 48 human players = 6 parties of 8. This does not mean each encounter requires 48 players or every room must accommodate everyone at once.

Players start in the Safe Zone, explore the blue zone, complete introductory quests and return to the entrance NPC. They then take the right-side route to the Checkpoint, continue through the red-zone quests, explore the inner catacomb, assemble a seal and unlock personal access to the boss room. Turning in the boss quest at the Checkpoint ends the floor 1 Quest Line.

Intended experience:

  • Blue zone: learn targeting, combat and exploration through basic enemies.
  • Red zone: read frontal attacks, LOS, slows and ground hazards; handle Elites and mixed packs.
  • Allow preparation, cover / elevation advantages and pre-clearing support packs rather than enforcing one tactic.
  • Include difficult packs that encourage calling for help. Map player counts are initial recommendations, not entry requirements or proven balance results.
  • Optional Rite and Lost Chamber propose alternatives off the Main Route so parties have less reason to converge on one location. Reduced clustering is not yet demonstrated.

Important before implementation: final-room Large Skeleton = Gravewarden 1F is confirmed. Remap content to the new LD base; agree Aggro Link, four nameplate anchors, personal boss access and respawn. Core direction is WASD and 1+3/fake2, avoiding True Projectile rather than banning all ranged visuals.

  1. Accept / turn in: quests may be accepted individually or together. Every quest in a set must be completed AND turned in to unlock the next set. Acceptance, progress and turn-in are personal. Objectives generally count after acceptance; simultaneous turn-in is unnecessary.
  2. Kills: party members need not attack personally but must have the quest and be in range when the enemy dies. Other participating parties may qualify; no exclusive Last Hit. Define participation and credit radius with Dev; tune range after testing.
  3. Drops: eligible kill-credit recipients with the quest receive personal drops, 100%, 1 item per enemy. Manual loot; stop at required count; consume on turn-in. Loot rules must define whether uncollected ground drops count toward the cap.
  4. Ground nameplates: personal collection, once per location per quest; use distinct locations. A pickup does not disappear for other players. Verify at least 4 eligible anchors in each quest area.
  5. Shrines: personal interaction at specified points; no repeat credit for one shrine. Another player's lit state cannot block your quest.
  6. Checkpoint / death: personal activation changes respawn. NPC turn-in is separate. Death preserves quest progress and items.
  7. Boss: personal entry rights, not a server-wide room toggle. Credit follows the Set 4 conditions. Dead players remaining in the area may qualify; respawning outside range does not. No quest-triggered boss spawn / reset.

Personal credit and loot do not make enemies personal: the world remains shared. Respawn must still support the population target.
